Buying guide

Choose the purifier for the room, not the promise.

In a bedroom, the useful questions are simple: can it move enough air, stay quiet enough to sleep beside and remain affordable to run?

By Habitat Journal editors·July 18, 2026·7 min read

Air purifiers are often sold through dramatic claims, but the machine has a modest job: move room air through the right filter often enough to make a meaningful difference. For a small bedroom, that makes sizing, sound and upkeep more important than a long list of app features.

Start with the room, including its height

Measure the floor area and note whether the ceiling is higher than the standard eight feet. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency recommends choosing a clean air delivery rate, or CADR, large enough for the space. Its reference table suggests a minimum smoke CADR of 130 cfm for a 200-square-foot room and 195 cfm for 300 square feet.

Those figures are an estimate rather than a universal promise. Open doors, tall ceilings and an adjoining dressing area increase the volume of air the machine must serve. Slightly oversizing the purifier can also let it run at a quieter middle speed instead of its loudest setting.

Judge noise at the speed you will actually use

A low sleep-mode number is useful, but it does not tell you how the purifier sounds while doing substantial work. Look for published noise figures across several fan speeds. A steady low hum is usually easier to live with than an irregular motor tone or electronic chime.

The best bedroom purifier is not the most powerful machine. It is the one you will leave running.

Separate particles from gases

Particle filters and gas filters do different jobs. A particle filter can address dust, pollen and smoke particles. Activated carbon or another adsorbent material is needed for gases and some odors, and the amount of that material matters. A thin carbon sheet should not be treated as equal to a substantial carbon bed.

Price the replacement filter, not only the machine

Check the official replacement interval, current filter availability and annual cost. A bargain purifier with a proprietary filter that is frequently unavailable is not a bargain. Also consider whether the pre-filter can be cleaned easily and whether the display can be fully dimmed at night.

Research note

This guide is based on public performance guidance, not a hands-on laboratory test. See the EPA Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home and ENERGY STAR product criteria.

What we prioritize

Correct room sizing, tolerable sound, honest filter costs and controls that disappear at night.
