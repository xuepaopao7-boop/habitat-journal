Under-cabinet light should illuminate the work, not itself.

A useful kitchen light disappears behind the cabinet edge, reaches the front of the counter and keeps glare out of the room.

By Habitat Journal editors·July 27, 2026·6 min read

Under-cabinet lighting is easy to overcomplicate. The goal is not to create a glowing strip visible from across the room. It is to place even light on the counter where chopping, reading and cleaning happen, while allowing the fixture to recede.

Move the light toward the front edge

A strip mounted against the backsplash can create a bright band at the rear and leave the working edge dim. Moving it toward the front of the cabinet projects light across more of the counter. The cabinet lip or a slim channel can then hide the source from normal sightlines.

Use a diffuser

Visible points from an LED strip make reflective stone and glossy tile look harsh. An aluminum channel with a frosted diffuser softens those points, keeps the line visually continuous and helps the installation feel intentional.

Good task lighting makes the surface clearer without making the fixture louder.

Match the room before chasing brightness

Color temperature should sit comfortably beside the kitchen's other lighting. Warm residential rooms often feel coherent around 2700K to 3000K, while a slightly cleaner 3500K can suit a modern work-focused kitchen. More important than the exact number is avoiding a visibly blue strip beneath warm ceiling lights.

Plan control and power early

Hardwired lighting gives the cleanest result during a renovation. Plug-in systems are easier to add later but need a route for cable and a discreet power point. A dimmer is worth prioritizing: bright light helps with preparation, while a low setting becomes useful background light after dinner.

Before ordering

Measure every cabinet run, mark breaks at sinks and appliances, decide where drivers will live and test the color temperature beside the room's existing lamps. For mains wiring, use a qualified electrician.

The short list

Front-edge placement, a diffuser, compatible dimming and a concealed route for power.
