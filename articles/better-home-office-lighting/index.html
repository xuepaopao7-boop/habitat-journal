Workspace

The small lighting shift that changes a workday.

Good office light is layered, adjustable and designed around the work—not centred on a single bright ceiling fixture.

By Habitat Journal editors·July 28, 2026·5 min read

A home office can have an excellent desk and supportive chair yet remain strangely tiring. Often the missing piece is light. One strong source creates glare and deep shadows; a dim room asks the eyes to keep adjusting between a bright screen and a dark background.

The solution is not simply more brightness. It is a useful relationship between daylight, focused task light and softer illumination for the room around you.

Place the desk across the window

When possible, sit perpendicular to the main window. This provides broad daylight without placing a glowing rectangle directly behind the screen or in your line of sight. Right-handed writers may prefer daylight from the left, with the reverse for left-handed writers, to reduce hand shadows.

Sheer curtains or an adjustable blind can soften sharp afternoon sun while preserving useful ambient light.

Give task light a specific job

A task lamp should illuminate the keyboard, notebook or material you are handling without reflecting in the screen. An adjustable arm and shielded shade matter more than decorative complexity.

The best task light is bright where the work happens and quiet everywhere else.

Place the lamp to the opposite side of your writing hand and slightly forward of your shoulder. If it creates a bright hotspot, bounce it from a pale wall or choose a wider, diffused shade.

Light the background

A low lamp on a shelf, a shaded floor lamp or a soft wall wash reduces the contrast between the screen and the rest of the room. This can make evening work feel less severe and gives video calls a more natural background.

Change the room at the end of work

Lighting can also create a boundary. Use brighter, cooler task lighting during focused hours, then switch it off when work ends and leave only a warm ambient source. The physical change helps the same room return to domestic life.

A better workspace rarely requires a complete setup overhaul. Begin with the light you have, move it with purpose and add only the layer that is missing.

Quick test

Turn off the ceiling light. Add one source for the desk and another for the room behind it. Notice where glare disappears.
